# Public website standard

The first screen must explain what the program develops, its current stage and how to get in touch. Ground measurements and proposed flight capability must be clearly distinguishable.

1. The homepage gives a short program overview. Detailed physics, calculation files and document fingerprints belong on their respective secondary pages or GitHub.
2. Every numerical result has a defined scope, measurement basis and accessible limitation. Unverified detections are never presented as confirmed vessels.
3. Existing providers already automate analysis. Commercial value must be tested against a customer's existing workflow. No customer commitments or performance gains are invented.
4. Contact opens a choice, not an unexpected email application. The address can be copied, a Gmail draft opened or a different mail app selected. The site does not pretend to receive a message itself.
5. Main reading text is at least 16 px. Primary interactive controls target at least 44 px, labels are explicit, focus is visible and the design adapts to mobile widths. Motion respects reduced-motion preferences.
6. Actual data visualizations retain attribution and limitations. A concept illustration must not be presented as flight CAD or completed hardware.
7. Public code, documents and routes are validated before deployment. Historical PDF URLs remain intact, archived versions are labelled and previously published section links have a continuation path.
8. No analytics, new tracking, account registration or third-party form submission is added without a concrete product reason and an accurate privacy notice.

Current delivery is the existing static GitHub Pages site. Large research packages and original satellite scenes are retained separately from the public website.
